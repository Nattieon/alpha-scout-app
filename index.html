How it works

Lock or ride. After a player's game, the app compares that score with what the rest of his week is likely worth: his remaining games, the chance he sits, and any bench player who could take his spot. If the score beats it, lock.

Playing to win. When you're ahead, it locks a bit earlier to protect the lead. When you're behind, it takes more chances. Your opponent is worked out the same way, so both chances to win add up to 100%.

Locks you made. Sleeper doesn't show locks until the week is over, so the app guesses earlier ones and asks you to confirm. For your opponent, open his lineup and set them from what you see in Sleeper.

Safety cushion. Treats a lead smaller than this many points as not safe yet, so the advice plays it like you're behind.

Late swaps. An open spot can change players before each tip-off. A backup plan says when a later game should replace an early one.
