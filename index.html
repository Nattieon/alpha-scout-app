How the calls work & settings

Sit risk is per player, from his own log (last season + this one): how often he sits ordinary games, how often he rests one night of a back-to-back and which night, how much he has missed overall, and whether he's just back from a long absence. The two nights of a back-to-back are linked: once he plays night 1, a rest becomes more likely on night 2; if he sits night 1, night 2 is safe. Tap Sits B2Bs? on any player to mark him as resting back-to-backs (95%); it stays on until you turn it off. League defaults below fill in for players with little history. Bench swaps: any open slot can go to a bench player before his next game. The app picks the best player for each open slot for the rest of the week (positions respected, each player used once), so a lock bar is what the slot is worth with your whole bench available, and Swaps before tip-off lists the moves the calls assume you'll make. Your opponent gets the same treatment, so the win % is the same matchup from either side (yours + theirs = 100%). By the rules, only a player's latest game can still be locked; an earlier game counts only if you locked it back then. So the lineup picker does two jobs: record a lock you made at the time (any game, earlier ones included), or say his latest game isn't locked: either he started and it's still your call, or it was played from your bench and can't count (same as ticking "He wasn't in my starting lineup" on his card). Bar = what the slot is worth if you pass: his remaining games this week, each with its own projection (Rotowire via Sleeper, calibrated to how he's actually played), played optimally, including the chance he sits. An open slot takes his last game even if he doesn't play, so DNP risk on the final game lowers the bar most. Each player's spread comes from his own game log (last season + this one, in your league's scoring), so a boom-or-bust player gets a higher bar than a steady one with the same average, and every score shows its percentile among his recent games. In win probability mode the call can flip: with a safe lead it locks a little early to remove risk; when behind it passes to chase upside. Sleeper never shows locks in its public data while the week is on: each slot shows the player's latest game, and locks are applied only when the week is finalized (Monday morning). So for any earlier game nobody has marked, the app assumes a sensible manager: he locked the first game that beat what the rest of his week was worth. That guess is made the same way for both teams. For your team it asks Did you lock these?; for your opponent, copy his lock icons from Sleeper with the picker on each slot. Banked counts only locks that are certain or that you entered. Proj adds Sleeper's next-game projection for every slot that isn't locked (switch to expected final below); the win probability always uses the full model.
